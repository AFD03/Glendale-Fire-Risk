3D Visualization of Wildfire Risk in Glendale, CA

A hybrid geospatial dashboard combining 3D terrain modeling, building-level fire risk scoring, and 2D analytical visualizations.

Project Purpose

This project explores wildfire exposure across Glendale, CA by combining geospatial datasets, terrain analysis, and logic-based building risk modeling. The goal is to understand not only where wildfire risk is highest, but also which structures are most affected.

Why Glendale?

Glendale sits along the Verdugo Mountains, an area recognized for steep slopes, dense vegetation, and significant fire history. The city has experienced multiple near-miss wildfires, and its hillside neighborhoods place thousands of structures close to wildland-urban interface zones.

Methods Overview

Our approach integrates terrain factors (slope, aspect), vegetation zones, and structure-level attributes such as height and elevation. Risk categories were calculated using a weighted logic-based model, then visualized in 3D using QGIS2threejs and in 2D using Plotly dashboards.

Data Sources

  • USGS DEM — Surface elevation & terrain derivatives
  • CalFire Fire Hazard Severity Zones (FHSZ)
  • LARIAC5 Building Footprints & Elevation
  • Fire history records for LA County, Ventura County, and Angeles National Forest
  • NOAA drought index records

Key Findings

• High-risk structures cluster heavily along Glendale’s eastern hillsides.
• The steepest terrain (20°–90° slopes) strongly correlates with building risk categories 4–5.
• Historical fire frequency indicates increased activity around suburban fringe zones.
• Periods of severe drought align with regional spikes in acreage burned.

Model Summary (Non-Technical)

We computed fire risk using a point-based scoring framework: higher scores correspond to steeper slopes, southern-facing aspects, and high-vegetation hazard. Building risk is the combination of its terrain score and local exposure factors. Each structure is assigned a category from 1 (low) to 5 (extreme).
